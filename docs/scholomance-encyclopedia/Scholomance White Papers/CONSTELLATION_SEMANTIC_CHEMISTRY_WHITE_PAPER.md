### 11.3 Polysemy Pressure, The 18 Toxic Switches, & The Resurrection Anomaly
Why did 116 sentences parse under Oracle UPOS but fail under the Real Dictionary?
A monotonic chart parser should retain any derivation whose leaf tags are a subset of the dictionary. An empirical audit of the 116 sentences revealed that the failure splits into two fundamentally distinct classes:

```

  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### The Single-Sense Lexical Interference Assay $\mathcal{I}(g, d)$
For the 18 true polysemy-collapse cases, we computed the Single-Sense Interference Effect:
$$\mathcal{I}(g, d) = \text{Outcome}(g) - \text{Outcome}(g + d)$$

**Findings:**
1. **$100\%$ Single-Sense Toxic Switches ($18 / 18$):** In every single sentence, the failure was triggered by a **single distractor tag** acting alone ($\mathcal{I}(g, d) = 1$). No pairwise or multi-body combinations were required.
2. **The $n \to v$ Impostor Atom:** The disruption was $100\%$ concentrated on nominal-to-verbal shifts ($n \to v$), occurring **27 times across all 18 affected sentences** (1.5 hits/sentence). When a noun (`group`, `service`, `message`, `mail`, `care`) acquires a verb reading, it greedily acts as an impostor predicate, consuming adjacent arguments before the true noun phrase ($NP$) can close.

#### The Lexical Resurrection Anomaly (Why $+1 \to +2$ Distractors Restored Parses)
In the progressive distractor curve, adding distractor senses exhibited non-monotonicity: $k=1$ produced 81 roots, but $k=2$ resurrected to 82 roots (and several sentences parsed under $k=2$ that failed under $k=1$).

Forensic trace of the resurrected sentences revealed **Partial Lexical Saturation vs. Category Restoration**:
* **Example 1 (`"Well , he launched today ."`):**
  - $k=0$ (Gold): `Well` handled by punctuation closure $\to$ **PARSED**.
  - $k=1$ (+1 Distractor): SQLite lists `['a', 'r']`. $k=1$ selects only `['a']` (adjective). An adjective preceding a comma has no noun to modify and blocks the clause $\to$ **FAILED**.
  - $k=2$ (+2 Distractors): $k=2$ injects both `['a', 'r']`. The restored adverb `[r]` unblocks `ADV + COMMA -> S` $\to$ **RESURRECTED**.
* **Example 2 (`"I have called Mark Lay..."`):**
  - $k=1$: `have` gets `[n]` (noun, from SQLite `lemma_form`), blocking its auxiliary verb role $\to$ **FAILED**.
  - $k=2$: `have` gets `[n, v]`, restoring the verb reading $\to$ **RESURRECTED**.

#### Controlled Polysemy Pressure Curve vs. Natural Lexicon
- **Controlled Polysemy (18 True Cases, Gold Guaranteed):** Drops sharply from $100\%$ ($k=0$) to $0\%$ at $k=1$ (due to the presence of the single toxic $n \to v$ switch), proving a sharp phase-boundary threshold rather than linear decay.
- **Natural Real Dictionary:** Fails on the remaining 98 cases due to lexical recall deficits (missing gold tags), not combinatorial explosion.
